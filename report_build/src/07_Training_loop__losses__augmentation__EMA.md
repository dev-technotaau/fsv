# AREA: Training loop, losses, augmentation, EMA, calibration, post-processing, inference (Step 5)

## SUMMARY
The training subsystem is a single, well-engineered two-phase pipeline driven by training/train.py and configured entirely through configs/phase1.yaml (512px, full ~23K-row dataset, 120 epochs) and configs/phase2.yaml (1024px HQ subset, init from phase1 best, 70 epochs). The active production backbone is DINOv3 ViT-L/16 ("facebook/dinov3-vitl16-pretrain-lvd1689m") in BOTH configs (line 13); the "H+" / ~840M / ~1066M references appear only in stale comments and in TRAINING_GUIDE.md, never in the active model field. The CombinedLoss (training/losses.py) sums roughly a dozen weighted components — BCE (pos_weight 3.0, focal_gamma 2.0, OHEM top-25%), Dice, Tversky (a0.6/b0.4), boundary, Lovasz, connectivity, deep supervision (0.3), PointRend importance sampling (12544 pts), edge, CGM, and boundary-distance regression — with separate coarse/refined/aux applications. The loop adds RandAugment+CutMix+Mosaic+multi-scale augmentation, a subcategory-balanced sampler, AdamW with layer-wise LR decay + cosine warmup, bf16 AMP, grad accumulation (effective batch 16 in phase1), EMA (decay 0.999 phase1 / 0.9999 phase2), per-epoch EMA-swapped validation, and a final TTA+post-process+calibrated test eval. Post-training calibration fits a temperature scalar and per-subcategory IoU-optimal thresholds, and the post_process cascade (guided filter r4 -> morphology -> dense-CRF 5 iters -> connected-component cleanup) is applied at the final test eval and at inference. All files are CURRENT and mutually consistent; the only stale artifact is TRAINING_GUIDE.md's H+/RTX-3090 framing.

## NARRATIVE
## Step 5 — Training, losses, and inference

This is the part of the project where everything that came before (the labeled fence masks, the DINOv3 backbone, the Mask2Former decoder, the UNet3+-style refinement head) gets turned into an actual trained model. It's organized as a single, clean two-phase pipeline. One Python module — `training/train.py` — runs one phase per invocation, and the entire behavior of each phase is pinned down in a YAML file: `configs/phase1.yaml` (512px, the full ~23K-row dataset, 120 epochs) and `configs/phase2.yaml` (1024px on the high-quality subset, 70 epochs, initialized from phase 1's best checkpoint). Phase 1 maximizes data exposure at moderate resolution; phase 2 is a gentler fine-tune that recovers the boundary detail you can't preserve at 512.

One thing worth stating clearly up front, because the comments are misleading: the production backbone is **DINOv3 ViT-L/16** — `facebook/dinov3-vitl16-pretrain-lvd1689m` — and that's what both `phase1.yaml:13` and `phase2.yaml:13` actually load. The bigger "H+" model (`vith16plus`, ~840M params) shows up only in a leftover comment block at the top of phase1.yaml and throughout `TRAINING_GUIDE.md` (which still says "DINOv3-H+", "1066.1M total, 943.7M trainable", and targets an RTX 3090). Those are stale; the live config never references H+. The guide is still useful for the command-line recipes, but its model name and parameter counts should not be quoted to the client.

### The loss — about a dozen components, carefully balanced

The heart of the training signal is `CombinedLoss` in `training/losses.py`. It's a weighted sum of many components, and the weights have clearly been tuned through a painful series of "FIX" and "audit" rounds you can read in the inline comments. The components:

- **BCE** with three fence-friendly modes stacked together: a `pos_weight` of **3.0** (deliberately capped down from the auto-computed 9.84, which was causing recall-over-precision "blob" predictions), a **focal** term with `gamma=2.0` to concentrate loss on hard look-alike pixels, and **OHEM** keeping only the top **25%** highest-loss pixels per image. The OHEM implementation is careful to divide by total pixel count, not just the kept-k, so it doesn't silently inflate the BCE scale. Weight 1.0.
- **Dice** (region overlap), weight 1.0.
- **Tversky**, a recall-tilted Dice with `alpha=0.6 / beta=0.4` (FN penalized more than FP, but the gap was eased from the original 0.7/0.3 because too many things were already pushing recall). Weight 0.5 in phase 1, 0.7 in phase 2.
- **Boundary loss**, an edge-weighted BCE that up-weights pixels near GT boundaries (Sobel edge map dilated into a band). Weight 0.4 in phase 1 — halved from 0.8 after the per-component log showed it driving 25% of a loss spike at the LR transition — and 1.0 in phase 2 where boundary sharpness matters most.
- **Lovász hinge**, a differentiable IoU surrogate, weight 0.25 in phase 1 (also halved, for the same LR-ramp stability reason) and 0.7 in phase 2.
- **Connectivity**, an experimental soft-Euler topology penalty, deliberately tiny at 0.02 / 0.03 (the docstring warns to keep it in the 1e-3–1e-2 range; an earlier 0.1 was actively reinforcing blob bias).
- **Deep supervision** at weight **0.3**, applied to every intermediate Mask2Former decoder layer's mask except the last (scaled down hard from 1.0 after they realized ~75% of the gradient was coming from aux layers instead of the main mask).
- **PointRend importance sampling**, which is the actual Mask2Former training-time loss for BCE+Dice: instead of dense per-pixel loss, it samples **12544 points** (112²) per image, **75%** chosen by uncertainty (logit closest to zero) and 25% uniform, with a 3× oversample pool. This is both faster and more boundary-focused. It only activates in training mode.
- **Edge auxiliary loss** on the refinement head's edge prediction (BCE vs Sobel GT edges), weight 0.8 / 1.0, with `edge_loss_pos_weight=4.0` (cut from 8.0 because combined with pos_weight 3.0 it was promoting thin boundary false positives).
- **CGM** (Classification-Guided Module): a per-image fence/no-fence classifier whose loss gates inference-time suppression of predictions on non-fence images. Weight 1.0 in phase 1 (doubled because logs showed it flat-lining), 0.5 in phase 2. Importantly, its target is derived from the *actual* mask pixels after CutMix/Mosaic (train.py:1053), which fixes a subtle bug where pasting fence pixels into a "negative" image would otherwise teach the gate the wrong label.
- **Boundary-distance regression**: an L1 loss between a predicted signed-distance head and the true signed distance (computed with scipy's `distance_transform_edt`), clipped to ±50px (phase 1) / ±80px (phase 2) and normalized. Weight 0.3 / 0.4.

On top of these, the refinement head's losses are applied at *half weight* (bce/dice/boundary/tversky × 0.5), and there are additional deep-supervision terms for the iterative refinement loop (`refinement_iter_aux_weight` 0.35/0.2) and the UNet3+ full-scale side heads (`refinement_fds_weight` 0.5/0.4). The total is always accumulated in fp32 even under bf16 autocast, because bf16's 7-bit mantissa loses precision on small loss values.

### Augmentation

The augmentation stack is aggressive in phase 1 and gentler in phase 2. On the photometric side there's **RandAugment** with n=2 transforms at magnitude m=10 (the magnitude is auto-clipped to 6 in phase 2 to preserve fine detail). On the spatial/mixing side, a custom `MultiScaleCollator` in train.py does three things in order on each batch: a multi-scale resize (phase 1: random factor 0.75–1.0, down-only, capped at 1.0 after upscaling caused OOMs), then **4-way Mosaic** at p=0.15, then pair-wise **CutMix** at p=0.3 (alpha=1.0). The collator is careful about RNG: because DataLoader workers are persistent, it pulls fresh entropy from the per-worker-seeded numpy RNG on every call so the mix boxes differ across workers and epochs. Both Mosaic and CutMix correctly rewrite the per-image class label and blend the per-sample loss weights. Finally there's a **subcategory-balanced sampler** (WeightedRandomSampler, `alpha=0.5` sqrt-frequency, `min_count=50`) that oversamples rare positive subcategories like occlusion and complex-background, which the team turned on in both phases after data showed the refinement and CGM losses weren't learning on the natural distribution.

### Optimizer, schedule, AMP

Phase 1 uses plain **AdamW** (they switched away from 8-bit Adam after finding its moment quantization drifted on this 485M-param, 12-component-loss setup), base LR **1.5e-4** for the head and **1.5e-5** for the backbone (a 10× ratio, chosen to let the backbone adapt without destroying DINOv3's pretrained features), with **layer-wise LR decay** of 0.9 so earlier transformer layers get progressively smaller LR. Weight decay is 0.05 (zeroed for biases, norms, and positional/CLS/register tokens). The schedule is cosine with an **8-epoch warmup** from 1e-7. Gradients are clipped to norm **2.0** (loosened from 1.0, which was destroying magnitude info Adam needs). Training runs in **bf16 AMP** (no grad scaler needed for bf16; only fp16 gets one), with **grad accumulation of 2** on a batch size of 8 for an **effective batch of 16**. Phase 2 differs: 8-bit AdamW (falls back to regular AdamW if bitsandbytes is missing), LRs Goyal-scaled up 1.5× (head 7.5e-5, backbone 3.0e-6), decay 0.85, 4-epoch warmup, clip 1.0, batch 6 × accum 2 = effective 12.

### EMA and validation

An exponential moving average of the weights is maintained (`ModelEMA`), with **decay 0.999** in phase 1 (lowered from 0.9999 because the longer window was smearing val results over a chaotic trajectory) and 0.9999 in phase 2. The shadow weights live on CPU to save VRAM, and decay ramps up linearly from 0.5 during the warmup steps. Every epoch, validation runs under the **EMA-swapped** weights and restores the live weights afterward — and crucially, `best.pt` is saved with the EMA weights too, so the checkpoint we call "best" is literally the model that achieved the metric. Per-epoch validation deliberately skips TTA and post-processing (both slow). The best-tracking metric is dataset-pooled `val_iou`. Phase 2 additionally turns on **EMA self-distillation** (Mean Teacher, weight 0.3): the live model is pulled toward the EMA teacher's predictions, with the teacher run under eval()+no_grad and only on the first microbatch of each accumulation group to halve the cost.

### Final test eval, calibration, post-processing, inference

When training finishes, the pipeline reloads `best.pt` and does a full, honest test evaluation. First it **fits a temperature scalar** on the val set via LBFGS to minimize BCE on `sigmoid(logits/T)` (Guo et al. calibration; clamped to [0.1, 10]). Then it **sweeps per-subcategory thresholds** from 0.20 to 0.80 in 0.025 steps, picking the IoU-optimal threshold per fence subcategory (buckets with fewer than 20 samples fall back to a global best, then to 0.5). Both are stored in the checkpoint meta and applied at test time. Then it forces **TTA on** — scales [0.5, 0.75, 1.0, 1.25, 1.5], horizontal flip, plus photometric perturbations (brightness ±0.10, gamma 0.9/1.1) — and runs the **post-process cascade** so the reported numbers match the deployed pipeline. That cascade (in `post_process.py`) is: guided filter (radius 4, eps 1e-4) to smooth the probability map with image guidance, prob-space morphology closing (kernel 3, no erosion so thin pickets survive), DenseCRF (5 iterations, bilateral sxy=50/srgb=10/compat=12, tuned tighter for fences), and connected-component cleanup that drops blobs under 150px (phase 1) / 250px (phase 2) while keeping picket gaps. The same `binarize_threshold=0.5` is used by both the in-training eval and the cascade so metrics are comparable. Everything gets rolled into a `final_summary.json` and propagated back into every checkpoint.

Checkpointing is robust: atomic `.tmp`-rename writes, full state (model+optimizer+scheduler+scaler+EMA+RNG) in `best.pt`/`latest.pt`, a stripped weights-only `best_inference.pt` for shipping, a separate `ema.pt`, and the last 3 periodic snapshots. Resume restores everything and advances the epoch counter; phase-2 init loads weights non-strictly with a 5%-mismatch abort guard so an architecture drift fails loud instead of silently random-initializing half the model. Provenance (git SHA, library versions, host, GPU) is baked into every file. The standalone `infer.py` reads the self-describing checkpoint (no YAML needed), supports per-image and folder inference, sliding-window tiling for 4K customer photos, and multi-checkpoint probability-averaged ensembles. The whole subsystem is internally consistent and current; the only thing a client report must not echo verbatim is TRAINING_GUIDE.md's H+/3090 framing.
